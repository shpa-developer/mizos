How to Measure Yourself for Clothes

Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

Getting clothes tailored starts with measurements. Whether you're visiting a tailor for the first time, ordering custom clothing online, or just trying to figure out your size in a new brand, knowing your body measurements saves time, money, and the frustration of clothes that don't fit.

This guide covers every measurement you're likely to need — from chest to inseam — with clear instructions you can follow at home.

Before You Start

What to wear

Measure over thin, fitted clothing — a lightweight T-shirt and thin trousers, or just undergarments. Bulky clothes add centimetres that throw off your results. If your tailor specifically asks you to measure over a particular garment, follow their instructions instead.

What you'll need

  • A flexible fabric measuring tape (available at any sewing or craft shop for under 50 rupees).
  • A mirror, or someone to help you.
  • Something to record the numbers — a notebook, your phone, or an app like Mizos that's built for storing measurements.

General rules

  • Stand upright and relaxed. Don't tense muscles or suck in your stomach.
  • Keep the tape snug against your body but not tight — you should be able to slip one finger under it.
  • Always keep the tape parallel to the floor for circumference measurements.
  • Measure each area twice. If the numbers differ, measure a third time.

The 10 Key Body Measurements

1. Chest / Bust

Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your chest, under the arms, across the shoulder blades. Keep the tape level and don't pull it tight. For a detailed guide, see our article on how to take chest measurements.

2. Waist

Find your natural waistline — it's the narrowest part of your torso, usually about 2–3 cm above your belly button. Bend to one side; the crease that forms is your natural waist. Wrap the tape around this point, keeping it level.

Tip: Don't measure at your belt line unless your tailor specifically asks for it. Your belt might sit lower than your natural waist, and the two measurements can differ by 5+ cm.

3. Hips

Stand with your feet together. Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your hips and buttocks. This is usually 18–23 cm below your natural waist. Keep the tape level all the way around.

4. Shoulder Width

This is easiest with a helper. Measure from the edge of one shoulder to the edge of the other, across the back. The "edge" is where your shoulder meets the top of your arm — the point where a well-fitting shirt's shoulder seam would sit.

5. Sleeve Length

Bend your arm at a slight angle (about 15 degrees). Have someone measure from the shoulder point (where you measured shoulder width) down over the elbow to the wrist bone. The slight bend ensures the sleeve won't ride up when you move your arm.

6. Neck

Wrap the tape around the base of your neck, where a shirt collar would sit. You should be able to fit two fingers between the tape and your neck — a collar needs room to be comfortable.

7. Inseam

Measure from the crotch seam (where the inner leg seams of your trousers meet) straight down to the floor, or to where you want the trouser to end. The easiest method: measure a pair of well-fitting trousers laid flat, from the crotch seam to the bottom hem, along the inner leg.

8. Outseam (Trouser Length)

Measure from your natural waist down the outside of your leg to the floor (or to your desired trouser length). This tells the tailor the full length of the trouser from waistband to hem.

9. Thigh

Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your thigh, usually right at the crotch level. This measurement is important for trousers, especially slim-fit or tapered styles.

10. Arm Length (Kurta / T-shirt)

For half-sleeve garments, measure from the shoulder point down to mid-bicep (or wherever you want the sleeve to end). For kurtas and full-arm garments, the sleeve length measurement above works.

When to Re-Measure

Your body changes. Re-take your measurements:

  • Every 6 months as a general check.
  • After significant weight change (gain or loss of 3+ kg).
  • Before ordering expensive tailored garments — don't rely on a measurement from a year ago for a wedding suit.
  • When switching between metric and imperial tailors — re-measure rather than converting, as rounding errors add up.

Final Tips

  • Always give actual measurements, not your "size." A "medium" at one brand is a "large" at another. Tailors work with numbers, not labels.
  • Don't add ease. That's the tailor's job. If your chest is 40 inches, say 40 — not 42 "for comfort."
  • Note the unit. Inches and centimetres look similar in small numbers. Always write "in" or "cm" next to every measurement.
  • Measure with the same tape every time. Cheap tapes can stretch over time. If you replace your tape, cross-check the new one against a ruler.

Getting your measurements right is a 15-minute investment that pays off every time you buy clothes. Do it once, store it properly, and you'll never have to guess your size again.
