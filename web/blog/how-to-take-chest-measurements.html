How to Take Chest Measurements Accurately

Updated November 2026 · 5 min read

Whether you're ordering a shirt online, getting a suit tailored, or buying a gift for someone, an accurate chest measurement is the single most important number you need. Get it wrong by even an inch, and the garment will either feel too tight across the torso or drape like a sack.

This guide walks you through exactly how to measure your chest at home, what tools you need, and the mistakes that throw most people off.

What You'll Need

  • A flexible measuring tape (the soft, fabric kind used for sewing — not a metal construction tape).
  • A mirror or a second person to help. It's harder to keep the tape level behind your back when you're measuring yourself.
  • A thin, fitted T-shirt or no shirt. Measuring over a thick sweater will add inches you don't want.

If you don't have a sewing tape at home, you can wrap a piece of string around your chest and then measure the string against a ruler. It's not as convenient, but it works in a pinch.

Step-by-Step: Taking Your Chest Measurement

1. Stand naturally

Stand upright with your arms relaxed at your sides. Don't puff out your chest or suck in your stomach. You want the measurement to reflect how you actually stand, not a posed version of it. Breathe normally — don't hold your breath.

2. Find the fullest part of your chest

For most people, this is right at nipple level or just slightly above. If you have a larger build, the widest point may be slightly higher. The goal is to find where your chest is at its maximum circumference.

3. Wrap the tape around your body

Bring the measuring tape around your back, under your armpits, and across the fullest part of your chest. The tape should be:

  • Parallel to the floor — not dipping down at the back or riding up on one side.
  • Snug but not tight — the tape should touch your skin without compressing it. You should be able to slide one finger under the tape comfortably.
  • Flat against your body — no twists or folds in the tape.

4. Read the number

Look at where the end of the tape meets the rest of the tape at the front of your chest. Read the measurement in inches or centimetres. Don't round — if it reads 39.5 inches, write down 39.5 inches.

5. Measure twice

Take the measurement at least twice to confirm. If the numbers differ by more than half an inch, do it a third time and take the average.

Tip: If you're measuring someone else, stand behind them and read the tape at the front. Make sure the tape stays level by checking its position on their back in a mirror.

Common Mistakes That Ruin Chest Measurements

Measuring over bulky clothing. Even a regular button-down shirt can add half an inch. Wear a thin undershirt or measure bare-chested for the most accurate reading.

Pulling the tape too tight. A tight tape gives you a smaller number, which feels flattering but results in clothes that don't fit. The tape should rest on your body, not squeeze it.

Letting the tape sag in the back. This is the most common problem when measuring yourself. The tape dips below the shoulder blades and gives you a number that's 1–2 inches too large. Use a mirror or ask someone to check that the tape is level.

Measuring at the wrong time. Your body changes throughout the day. Measure in the morning before heavy meals, and after your regular exercise routine if you work out daily. Consistency matters more than the exact time — just pick a time and stick with it.

Confusing chest size with shirt size. A 40-inch chest doesn't mean you wear a size 40 shirt. Shirt sizing accounts for ease of movement, fabric type, and design. Always share your actual measurement with your tailor and let them handle the sizing.

Chest Measurement vs. Jacket/Suit Measurement

When a tailor asks for your "chest measurement" for a suit, they typically want the same measurement described above — around the fullest part of your bare chest (or over a thin shirt). The tailor then adds their own ease allowance based on the style of suit (slim fit adds less, relaxed fit adds more).

Don't try to add ease yourself. Give the actual number and let the tailor do the math.

Quick Reference: Chest Measurement Checklist

  1. Wear a thin shirt or measure bare-chested.
  2. Stand upright, arms at sides, breathing normally.
  3. Wrap tape around the fullest part of the chest, parallel to the floor.
  4. Keep the tape snug — one finger should fit underneath.
  5. Read the exact number without rounding.
  6. Measure twice to confirm.
  7. Record it somewhere you won't lose it.

That's it. A good chest measurement takes 30 seconds and saves you from ill-fitting clothes, return shipping, and the awkward conversation with your tailor when the shirt comes back too small. Measure once, measure right, and keep the number where you can find it.
